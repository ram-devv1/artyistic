# Odysseus theme contract v1.2

Build an immersive journey from Troy to Ithaca about survival after victory. Treat survivor's guilt as a modern interpretive lens, not a Homeric diagnosis. Keep Odysseus morally tense: cunning, responsible, proud, frightened, grieving, angry, and capable of vengeance.

## Source boundaries

- Use Homeric concealment, impossible choices, tears, crew loss, recognition, revenge, and imposed peace for the narrative.
- Use timber, bronze, wool, linen, rope, pitch, Mycenaean forms, and Late Bronze Age Aegean clothing as period constraints—not proof of a literal reconstruction.
- Use later art and modern cinema only for compositional research. Do not copy film assets, actors, costumes, posters, or production-specific typography.

## Four-act journey

1. **Troy:** victory is already grief. Show Odysseus leaving the burning shore and looking back rather than posing as a conqueror.
2. **The sea:** compress the voyage into command responsibility, exhausted bodies, lost ships, and the names one survivor carries. The voyage is not a monster catalogue.
3. **The dead:** stage the Book XI blood rite with Tiresias, the mother, companions, Achilles, or Agamemnon as human memory—not Christian hell or horror spectacle.
4. **Ithaca:** recognition arrives through Argos, the scar, Penelope, the bow, and the olive-root bed. Homecoming remains morally unsettled until Athena stops another cycle of blood.

Preserve that order on every viewport.

## Art direction

Create four original, full-bleed narrative masterworks before composing the interface:

| Act | Required composition | Emotional center |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Troy | Odysseus, black-hulled ship, surviving crew, distant fire; asymmetric text-safe smoke | victory curdling into guilt |
| Sea | Odysseus integrated among exhausted oarsmen on a physically believable ship | responsibility and endurance |
| Underworld | Odysseus guards the blood offering while Tiresias and human shades gather | dread, grief, memory |
| Ithaca | Argos recognizes the disguised king; Penelope, bow, loom, and olive bed remain quiet evidence | tenderness and recognition |

Use `imagery-language.md`. Favor physically made old-master materiality: charcoal or metalpoint construction, red-chalk corrections, thin oil or tempera glazes, scraped pigment, broken edges, stained fibers, and unfinished passages. Colors may change by act; craft, texture, restrained anatomy, and editorial treatment keep the series coherent.

Reject glossy fantasy posters, photoreal movie stills, game concept art, centered heroic poses, plastic anatomy, generic marble-and-gold Greece, vase collages, decorative Greek-key bands, or any image that merely depicts an object instead of carrying the emotion.

## Interface grammar

- Make artwork occupy 70–100% of each narrative viewport. Remove dashboard cards, stat counters, tickers, maps, and ornamental diagrams.
- Use one assertive old-world serif for emotional titles, one quiet sans for prose, and a mono face only for chapter marks and commands.
- Keep chrome nearly invisible. Use one ember-red semantic accent across the whole page.
- Place copy like art-book marginalia. Never cover the decisive face, hand, animal, or recognition object.
- Keep one primary action: invoke the theme.

## Motion grammar

Apply `motion-language.md`. Motion must feel physical and scroll-causal:

| Act | Motion | Reduced motion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Troy | slow camera push and slight copy retreat | static crop and visible copy |
| Sea | shallow vertical drift through canvas depth | static painting |
| Underworld | near-imperceptible descent into darkness | static painting |
| Ithaca | slow release from a tight crop toward recognition | static final crop |

Do not animate every element. No count-ups, moving markers, spinning symbols, card cascades, or decorative loops near reading.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] The four acts read in order and each has a distinct emotional job.
- [ ] Four original masterworks pass full-size anatomy, period, composition, and generated-image-tell review.
- [ ] Art remains primary on desktop and mobile; typography never clips or competes with the focal subject.
- [ ] Argos, Penelope, the bow, and olive bed serve recognition rather than decoration.
- [ ] Motion is shallow, compositor-friendly, optional, and nonessential to meaning.
- [ ] Keyboard focus, contrast, alt text, anchor navigation, reduced motion, build, and small-screen layout pass.
