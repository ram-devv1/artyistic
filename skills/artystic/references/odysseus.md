# Odysseus theme contract v1.1

Build an immersive, legible journey from Troy to Ithaca. The thesis is *nostos*: a king returns after twenty years carrying the dead, grief, command responsibility, cunning, pride, pain, anger, and revenge. Survival did not leave him innocent, whole, or finished.

## Source boundaries

- **Homeric narrative:** use concealment, impossible choices, tears, loss of the crew, recognition, vengeance, and imposed peace as the textual story layer.
- **Bronze Age material:** use timber, bronze, iron, bone, wool, linen, rope, pitch, palace weight, and Mycenaean forms as era-near material culture, not proof of a literal Homeric scene.
- **Later Greek visual reception:** use vase bands, ships, bird-woman Sirens, monster forms, and object compositions as later interpretations, not documentary views of Troy.
- **Original modern composition:** synthesize those layers with editorial scale, crop, light, type, and pacing; label modern cinema only as atmosphere, never historical evidence.
- Treat survivor's guilt as a modern interpretive lens, never a Homeric diagnosis. Keep Odysseus morally tense: neither innocent victim nor simple villain.

## Six-act narrative grammar

1. **After Troy:** victory appears as smoke, ash, burnt bronze, and absent names.
2. **The sea takes the fleet:** compress the voyage into loss and responsibility, not a monster sightseeing map.
3. **Cunning under pressure:** stage Nobody, the blinded eye, ram, cup, mast, rope, and Scylla's cost.
4. **The dead speak:** let Achilles and Agamemnon remain underworld shadows; include grief, counsel, the mother, and absent companions without Christian-hell imagery.
5. **Ithaca does not recognize him:** use disguise, Argos, scar, Athena's mist, and Penelope's strategic weaving as quiet tests of identity.
6. **The bow / the bed / peace:** align the bow and twelve axe apertures, make revenge and anger morally heavy, root recognition in Penelope's olive bed, and let Athena stop the next cycle of blood.

Preserve this order on every viewport. Sections may vary in size, but none may become a decorative crop or generic card feed.

## Visual system

Use a cold, time-worn palette:

| Token | Value | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold black | `#090D0F` | page, hull, underworld |
| Ash | `#555B59` | secondary planes and muted copy |
| Bone | `#D8CCB4` | primary text, sail, warm light |
| Oxidized green | `#41645D` | salt-aged metal and sea signals |
| Muted brick | `#A24A31` | terracotta and restrained emphasis |
| Blood | `#7B211F` | one withheld semantic accent at the reckoning |

- Set display titles and real Greek labels in **GFS Didot**; never fake Greek with substituted Latin glyphs.
- Set navigation and readable copy in **IBM Plex Sans**. Set coordinates, source labels, and command syntax in **IBM Plex Mono**.
- Compose authored archival collage from rights-cleared museum material, decisive crops, duotone separation, ink/grain, and original cartographic marks. It must not resemble AI concept art.
- Treat Penelope, Argos, Athena, the bow and axes, the olive bed, Achilles, and Agamemnon as narrative evidence, never mascots or decorative icon inventory.

## Component grammar

- Use sharp or very low-radius panels, solid rules, bowstring lines, vase-band separators, and deliberate asymmetry.
- Give each viewport one dominant image or idea. Avoid generic equal card grids and empty right-side image boxes.
- Use one clear primary action. Buttons are solid, low-radius, at least 44px high, and concise; secondary actions are quiet text or rule treatments, not pills.
- Keep navigation compact and readable. Keep source metadata subordinate to narrative copy.

## Motion budget

Use exactly two meaningful motion moments:

1. Draw one voyage route across the sea passage.
2. Tighten the final bowstring once at the invocation.

Keep every other state static. Use native CSS, DOM, and SVG; do not add an animation or video runtime. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, show the complete route and final states immediately.

## Accessibility and performance

- Keep body copy at least 16px, controls at least 44px, visible keyboard focus, semantic landmarks, logical headings, and WCAG AA contrast.
- Keep critical text out of imagery and write meaningful alt text for narrative images.
- Preserve content and narrative order on mobile; prevent horizontal overflow.
- Use responsive image dimensions and formats, reserve media space to avoid layout shift, and keep animation compositor-friendly.
- Make the complete experience usable with scripts, hover, and animation unavailable.

## Refusal rules

- Refuse copied film assets, actor likenesses, trailer frames, costumes, ships, posters, or title treatments. Do not imitate a living production's look.
- Refuse AI concept-art aesthetics, invented heroic portraits, generic marble-and-gold Greece, faux-Greek text, and decorative antiquity cosplay.
- Refuse claims that collapse Homeric narrative, Bronze Age material, later reception, and modern composition into one archaeological reconstruction.
- Refuse a clinical diagnosis attributed to Homer, triumphant game-like vengeance, mascot Argos, mermaid Sirens, fiery Christian Hades, or motion beyond the two-budget moments.
- Refuse inaccessible contrast, hidden focus, embedded critical copy, or animation-required meaning even when requested as polish.
